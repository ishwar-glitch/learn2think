# U-GL-2 — Stridelab (subscription fitness app)
**Context:** GLOBAL   **Currency:** USD   **Industry:** Subscription fitness app   **Name status:** fictional; trademark check needed [VERIFY]

## Story
Stridelab is a fitness app with guided workouts, programmes and a small trainer community. Founded in 2016, it grew on free trials and steep discounts. Many members join on an offer and leave when the price returns. Leadership suspects discounts buy short-lived members, but marketing insists they drive growth.

## Business model
Free app with a paid subscription. Members pay monthly ($12.99) or annually ($79.99). New members often start on a discounted introductory offer, and lapsed members are offered come-back deals. Revenue is subscriptions only.

## Canon facts
1. Founded 2016, headquartered in Denver. About 410 employees; 45 in-house trainers.
2. 14 million free registered users; 1.8 million paying subscribers: 900,000 monthly plan, 900,000 annual plan.
3. Prices: $12.99 a month or $79.99 a year (list price). Annual recurring revenue about $212 million (monthly plan about $140 million, annual plan about $72 million).
4. Monthly plan churn 9.5% of subscribers a month. Annual plan renewal 58%.
5. About 1.6 million new subscribers a year; 38% (about 608,000) joined on a discounted introductory offer.
6. Six-month retention: discounted joiners 31%, full-price joiners 52%.
7. 7-day free trial converts to paid about 24%.
8. 1,200 workouts in the library. About 60% of active members use the app at least twice a week.
9. Gross margin 81% (content and payment fees are the main costs). Marketing is about 30% of revenue.
10. Growth channels: paid social 45% of new subscribers, app-store search 25%, referrals 15%, partnerships 15%.

## Strengths and weaknesses (case seeds)
- **Strengths:** strong content; loyal annual members; rich usage data.
- **Weaknesses:** discount-driven growth; high monthly churn; dependence on paid social; unclear link between offers and lasting members.
- **Seeds:** retention decline; funnel drop between trial and paid; A/B readout that should not ship; attribution of new members; cutting discounts (DA capstone).
