# U-IN-4 — Kesari Naturals (family-owned personal-care brand)
**Context:** IN   **Currency:** ₹   **Industry:** Family-owned direct-to-consumer (D2C) personal care   **Name status:** fictional; trademark check needed [VERIFY]

## Story
Kesari Naturals began in 1962 in Kolhapur as a hair-oil maker sold through local shops. The third generation launched a website in 2019 and now sells hair oils, shampoos and skincare online as well as through distributors. The family still owns the company and makes decisions together, which slows some choices and protects the brand.

## Business model
Kesari makes its own products in one factory, then sells through three routes: its own website and app (D2C), online marketplaces, and offline distributors to shops. Margin is highest on its own site; marketing cost is highest there too.

## Canon facts
1. Founded 1962 in Kolhapur. Headquartered in Pune. One factory in Kolhapur. Three generations in the business; the promoter family owns 100%.
2. Revenue ₹210 crore a year: own website and app ₹94.5 crore (45%), marketplaces ₹52.5 crore (25%), offline distributors ₹63 crore (30%).
3. 38 products (SKUs). Hair oil is about 55% of revenue, shampoo 25%, skincare 20%.
4. Own-site average order value ₹690, so about 13.7 lakh orders a year.
5. Blended gross margin 62%. Marketing is 28% of revenue.
6. Cost to acquire a new own-site customer (CAC) ₹520. 34% of own-site customers order again within six months.
7. Marketplaces take about 22% commission on sales [VERIFY typical range]. Distributors are given about 18% margin.
8. Ships to 12 countries; international is about 3% of revenue.
9. Factory runs at about 78% of capacity. Raw materials (oils, herbs) are bought from farmers in three states; prices swing seasonally.
10. About 620 employees; 360 in the factory.

## Strengths and weaknesses (case seeds)
- **Strengths:** trusted heritage brand; owned factory and control of quality; loyal older customers.
- **Weaknesses:** low repeat rate online; dependence on marketplace and discounts; slow, family-based decisions; limited data skills.
- **Seeds:** go global or double down at home; discount and marketing spend; channel conflict between own site and distributors; ageing customer base; new product launch.
